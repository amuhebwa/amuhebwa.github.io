I am an Applied Scientist at **Amazon**, where I build production LLM systems for sustainability and responsible-AI deployment. My current work powers near-real-time human-rights and carbon-risk monitoring across 350,000+ global suppliers — turning what used to be 24-month manual audit cycles into continuous, automated intelligence.

Before Amazon, I was a Postdoctoral Researcher at **Stanford University** with [Prof. Khalid Osman](https://profiles.stanford.edu/khalid-osman), where I was awarded a $100K Precourt Institute grant to develop frameworks for principled accuracy-vs-emissions tradeoffs in AI deployment. I completed my **PhD in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Massachusetts Amherst** under [Prof. Jay Taneja](https://stima.ecs.umass.edu/), as a [UN-IPCC Scholar (2021–2023)](https://www.ipcc.ch/) and [e-GUIDE](https://eguide.io/) fellow. I hold an MS in ECE from **Carnegie Mellon University** and a BS in Computer Engineering from **Makerere University**.

My research sits at the intersection of applied ML, responsible AI, and large-scale infrastructure. I work on **production LLM pipelines, model interpretability, energy-aware AI, and federated learning optimization** — with a recurring throughline: making ML systems trustworthy enough to deploy in high-stakes domains where the cost of a wrong answer is real. For a full list of publications, see my [Google Scholar profile](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=8mdZdQTo2SYC&hl=en) (h-index 7, 260+ citations).
